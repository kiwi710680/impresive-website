Case 01 · Cardiovascular

ASCVD risk stratification remained informative in East Asian data

The programme assessed whether the 2018 AHA/ACC/Multi-society Guideline stratification separated hospitalized ASCVD patients across the represented East Asian settings and examined consistency before and after common-data-model conversion.

Population
Hospitalized ASCVD
Comparison
Very-high-risk vs high-risk
Outcome
ASCVD recurrence
Routes tested
OMOP and Sentinel

Why this question

A clinical question and an implementation test

  1. The scientific question

    Does the 2018 AHA/ACC/Multi-society stratification transfer?

    The guideline separates patients at very high risk of recurrent atherosclerotic cardiovascular events from those at high risk. The study examined whether that stratification was transferable to East Asian settings.

  2. The engineering question

    Are results consistent across implementation routes?

    The programme compared a conventional protocol-based analysis with common-data-model results in ASCVD cohorts from Taiwan and Korea.

  3. Why both at once

    One case, two complementary questions

    The case evaluated the clinical stratification and the consistency of results before and after conversion without treating common-data-model use as proof of validity by itself.

What came back

The risk gradient held, while treatment intensity remained limited

Reported hazard-ratio range

1.2 – 1.6

Recurrent ASCVD in the very-high-risk group relative to the high-risk group. The very-high-risk group remained clinically distinct, and risk stratification was transferable across the represented East Asian settings.

High-intensity statin use during hospitalization

18% – 34%

Treatment intensity does not match risk. Between 18% and 34% of patients were prescribed high-intensity statins during hospitalization.

The method question

Does converting the data change the answer?

The programme compared results before and after common-data-model conversion to identify where implementation could change the answer.

  1. The design

    Same question, two paths, two countries

    Results produced by a conventional protocol-based analysis were compared against results produced through common-data-model conversion, in the ASCVD cohort in Taiwan and Korea.

  2. The result

    Broadly consistent, with patterned exceptions

    Overall consistency held between the two approaches. Where inconsistencies appeared, they were variable-specific rather than systematic.

  3. The cause

    Terminology mapping was the main source of distortion

    The source identifies terminology mapping as the main source of distortion, emphasizing the need to review the variables on which each study depends.

  4. The consequence

    Fit-for-purpose implementation is essential

    A common data model improves reproducibility. It does not remove the need to validate, in every database, that the specific variables a study depends on survived conversion intact.
