Partner context

Different data environments, one readiness language

IMPRESIVE connects national claims, hospital electronic records, and other approved data settings. Partners are described by research context and suitability—not ranked by database size.

Four readiness questions

What makes a database ready for a study?

Data readiness

Can the environment identify the target population, exposure, comparator, outcome, covariates, time zero, and follow-up?

  • Variable availability
  • Coverage and observation time
  • Fit-for-purpose CDM status

Method readiness

Can the common study design be implemented and checked consistently?

  • Protocol and code lists
  • Variable definitions
  • QA and analysis programs

Collaboration readiness

Can the local team and governance processes support distributed research?

  • Local PI and analysts
  • IRB and data-owner review
  • Output sharing and revision

Interpretation readiness

Can results be understood in their population and healthcare context?

  • Coding and clinical practice
  • Coverage and comparability
  • Heterogeneity and transportability

The instrument behind the readiness check

Eight source-data domains, assessed before any conversion begins

The programme's source data survey classifies each required column as directly available, obtainable through derivation, or not available. The framework and its documented derivations are published below. Per-partner results are not — those belong in governed partner workflows.

Source data survey framework: target domains, required columns, and derivations the programme documented
Target domainRequired columnsTypical availabilityDocumented derivation where needed
DemographicPerson identifier, sex, year of birthUsually directDocument whether population descriptors are directly recorded or assumed; do not present an assumption as measured data.
EnrollmentPerson identifier, enrollment date, disenrollment datePartly derivedDisenrollment commonly derived as the last date in the database or the death date.
EncounterPerson identifier, visit type, visit start and end datesPartly derivedVisit end date derived by adding hospitalized days where the source records only an admission date.
DiagnosisPerson identifier, diagnosis code, diagnosis dateUsually directCoding systems differ by site — ICD-9-CM, ICD-10-CM, and KCD-7 all appear across the network.
PrescribingPerson identifier, drug code, treatment start and end datesPartly derivedDrug end date derived by adding drug days. Drug codes are typically domestic rather than international.
ProcedurePerson identifier, procedure code, procedure dateUsually directProcedure coding mixes international and domestic systems depending on the environment.
Laboratory resultPerson identifier, laboratory data, examination dateVaries by siteAvailability differs substantially between claims and hospital record environments.
DeathPerson identifier, death date, cause of deathVaries by siteCause of death is not available in every environment even where the death date is.

Directly available in the sourceRequires derivation, calculation, or additional data

Temporal granularity is a separate question.

Two environments can both carry a diagnosis date and still be incompatible for a study, because one records exact dates and another records only the month. That distinction constrains exposure windows, outcome ascertainment, and analytic time windows, and it is assessed alongside the domains above.

Study-level decision

Readiness is not a permanent badge

The same data environment may be ready for one question and unsuitable for another. The decision must be revisited whenever the protocol, data period, definitions, or local environment changes.

Minimum feasibility record

  • Question and intended use
  • Required populations, variables, and follow-up
  • Local source and transformation assumptions
  • Quality findings and unresolved limitations
  • Governance, disclosure, and execution constraints
  • Interpretation plan for expected differences
