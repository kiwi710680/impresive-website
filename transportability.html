Case 03 · Methodological extension

Evidence travels from Taiwan to Japan

A proof-of-concept study compares observed estimates in Taiwan NHIRD and Japan DeSC with estimates transported by a model developed in Taiwan.

Design
Retrospective target-trial emulation
Settings
Taiwan and Japan
Comparisons
Three antiplatelet trials

Study aim

A feasibility test for cross-country evidence transport

To evaluate the feasibility of transporting evidence across countries through a proof-of-concept study built on prior IMPRESIVE research, with the goal of informing healthcare decision-making.

The question is not “are the databases identical?”

The study asks whether a model developed in one setting can recover the estimate observed in another after accounting for measured differences in the target population.

Method

Three cohort studies using a target-trial emulation framework

Hospitalized patients with acute coronary syndrome are studied separately in each database. The three head-to-head comparisons are prasugrel versus clopidogrel, ticagrelor versus clopidogrel, and ticagrelor versus prasugrel. Permitted aggregate results are then synthesized for cross-database interpretation.

Taiwan

NHIRD

Population-based national claims environment used to develop the source model and estimate observed treatment effects.

Japan

DeSC

Population-based claims environment used to estimate the observed target-setting effect and evaluate the transported estimate.

Interpretation boundaries

Differences are the result to explain, not an error to hide

Absolute-risk heterogeneity

Baseline risk can differ substantially across healthcare contexts. Coding intensity, patient selection, and utilization patterns must be examined before transporting absolute estimates.

Relative-effect inconsistency

A relative measure is not automatically transportable. Directional differences may indicate that measured or unmeasured effect modifiers are distributed differently.

Effect-modifier discrepancies

Comorbidities can have different interaction patterns, and rare outcomes can produce wide uncertainty. Calibration must be evaluated outcome by outcome.
