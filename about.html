About IMPRESIVE

Paving the Way to Robust Global Evidence

Starting from common protocol approaches, the IMPRESIVE framework has evolved into a structured program encouraging the implementation of Common Data Model (CDM) standards.

A complete pathway from databases to decisions

International Multi-database study PREparedness: Databases Standardization, Integration and Visualization for timely Evaluation on Disease and Treatment.

The programme prepares local data environments for common study questions, transparent implementation, quality review, distributed analysis, and contextual interpretation. It does not require person-level partner data to be centralized.

Our work spans the entire research pipeline, from feasibility assessment, protocol and variable specification, and data standardization (CDM implementation) to quality validation, common analytical programming, distributed analysis, and cross-database interpretation.
We provide the methods, reusable code, quality-assurance processes, and analytical support required to transform research questions into decision-ready evidence, while person-level data remain securely under local control.

Why collaborate across data environments?

  • Diverse Populations & Settings: Evidence from a single database may not generalize to populations in other regions. Differences in reimbursement systems, care pathways, and prescribing practices vary significantly across databases, directly impacting measured health outcomes.
  • Feasibility & Precision: Aggregating multiple data sources enables investigations into rare events, rare exposures, or small patient subgroups. This improves statistical precision and yields more reliable treatment effect estimates when definitions and follow-up periods are standardized.
  • Consistency & Heterogeneity: Cross-country comparisons reveal whether diagnosis and prescribing patterns are globally consistent or where clinically and operationally meaningful variations exist.
  • Transportability: Multi-database analyses assess whether evidence generated in one setting can reliably inform clinical and policy decisions in another.

      1. The work

        A multinational common-protocol collaboration

        The BTA project studied utilization patterns and effectiveness of bone-targeting agents in breast, prostate, and lung cancer with bone metastasis. A standardized protocol was delivered to each data partner.

      2. The cost

        Alignment consumed the effort

        Substantial work was required to align protocol details, ensure appropriate variable definitions at every site, and independently develop analytic programs at each location.

      3. The decision

        Move the standardization upstream

        The next programme stages moved from protocol-only alignment toward common data models and shared analytic programs, while retaining local implementation and review.

      Aim. Examine whether treatment-effect estimates from Taiwan can inform a Japanese setting without treating population differences as noise.

    The Advantages of the Common Data Model (CDM)

    Upfront Standardization for Long-Term Analytical Efficiency

    A Common Data Model reorganizes local data into a shared structure and set of definitions. This allows identical analytical code to run across all participating sites while patient-level data remain protected inside local firewalls. While standardization requires an initial investment, once databases are mapped and validated, that infrastructure is reused seamlessly across subsequent studies.

    Why CDM?

    Research infrastructure

    IMPRESIVE

    IMPRESIVE is the underlying infrastructure ensuring study-preparedness, standardized structures, reusable analytical programs, and seamless evidence workflows.

    Internal rules:

    • Proposal & scope review: intended uses, roles, feasibility, and priorities are thoroughly evaluated before any project execution begins.
    • Local data control: our framework guarantees that person-level data remain within approved local environments, sharing only aggregate, privacy-compliant outputs.
    • Ethics & regulatory approval: we strictly follow applicable ethics committees, privacy laws, data-owner requirements, and institutional guidelines.
    • Authorship & publication governance: we ensure clear credit allocation, peer-review responsibilities, and publication expectations are established prior to public release.
    • Conflict-of-interest management: we disclose relevant interests through the research cycle.
