The central finding

How much does the definition move the answer?

The programme applied three definitions of increasing specificity and compared the resulting prevalence estimates across participating environments.

D1Any diagnosis
D2Two diagnoses within 180 days
D3Two diagnoses within 180 days plus systemic AD/PN medication

Database-specific rule: for CDARS, D3 is any diagnosis plus systemic AD medication — an accommodation the framework carries rather than overrides.

What the comparison showed

Prevalence falls substantially as the definition tightens in every database that reported all three definitions. A single diagnosis code admits a much larger population than a definition requiring repeated diagnoses plus systemic treatment.

The comparison shows why operational definitions must be reported explicitly: estimates can differ because teams apply different diagnosis-frequency or treatment requirements.

The second finding

Cross-environment variation differed by outcome

The programme reported three clinical outcomes as incidence rates per person-year with confidence intervals. They behave very differently from one another, and that pattern is itself the result.

Interpretation boundary

The observed variation should not be attributed to a single cause from these results alone. Differences may reflect population characteristics, coding practices, data coverage, clinical practice, or local implementation. The reported rates describe the cross-environment variation but do not establish which mechanisms produced it.

How flexibility was preserved

Standardization did not require erasing local coding

  1. Shared

    The data structure, the modules, and the quality standard

    The programme used a common-data-model structure, shared analysis modules, and common quality expectations to support comparable outputs.

  2. Local

    The source code lists behind each concept

    A coding library carries site-specific definitions. Ischemic stroke, for example, is I63 in NHIRD, CGRD, and DeSC; I63 and I64 in NHIS and HIRA; and 433 and 434 in CDARS. The framework does not pretend these coding systems are identical.

  3. Negotiated

    The disease algorithm, where local practice requires it

    CDARS uses a different D3 rule. That is recorded and applied rather than forced into conformity, because forcing it would produce a comparable-looking number that means something different.

The conclusion reported by the programme

The CDM approach was feasibly applied to an additional therapeutic area; disease burden, standard of care, and clinical outcomes were consistently characterized; and flexible disease-defining algorithms were accommodated within the framework.
