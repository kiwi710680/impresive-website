The rationale

One country cannot answer every real-world question

International multi-database studies can increase feasibility and precision while testing whether findings hold across different populations, health systems, and clinical practices.

Four reasons

Why collaborate across data environments?

1

Diverse populations and settings

Evidence from one country may not represent other populations, reimbursement systems, care pathways, or prescribing practices.

2

Feasibility and precision

Multiple data sources can support rarer events, subgroups, and more precise estimates when definitions and follow-up are suitable.

3

Consistency and heterogeneity

Cross-country comparison reveals whether patterns are consistent and where clinically or operationally meaningful differences appear.

4

Transportability

Comparing contexts helps teams judge whether evidence may inform decisions beyond the setting in which it was first generated.

Distributed by design

The analytic program travels—not the source data

Local partners work within their approved secure environments. Only outputs permitted by local governance are returned for review and integration.

What is shared

Common question, protocol, specifications, analysis program, QA logic, and agreed output structure.

What stays local

Person-level source data, local access controls, data-owner approvals, and environment-specific implementation detail.

What returns

Permitted aggregate results, QA summaries, implementation notes, and limitations needed for interpretation.

Interpret differences

Different results are not automatically errors

A difference can arise from source data, coding, cohort implementation, population characteristics, healthcare delivery, or treatment practice.

The preparation question

Before integrating results, determine whether a difference is a correctable data or implementation issue—or a finding that needs clinical and system-level explanation.

Method resources

Release findings only after content and ownership are verified

The public site can explain the ETL process, structural mappings, quality framework, and approved evidence data. Protocols, code lists, QA tools, and analytic code need separate owner, version, governance, and maintenance review before public release.

Current release status

  • Public now: method descriptions, mapping examples, evidence figures, and figure data
  • Pending review: downloadable templates, code lists, protocols, and QA materials
  • Future system: a governed repository or member workspace
